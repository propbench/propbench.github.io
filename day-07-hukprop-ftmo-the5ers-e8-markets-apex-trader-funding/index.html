Today’s five desks

How long the argument has lasted

A young page can be honest. An old page can be stale. What age gives you is strangers who already fought with the rulebook in public. Today’s ages belong to HUKProp, FTMO, The5ers, E8 Markets and Apex Trader Funding. Where a desk did not print a founding year, we say so instead of inventing one.

So today really is three sports: a CFD desk, a futures desk, and a one-day gold card. Do not average them into one score.

The desks, one by one

HUKProp

Gold only, XAU/USD, one day · Public page read on 2 Nov 2026. Founded in 2022, per the firm.

A one-day gold challenge with tiny lot caps. Not an 80 percent split of the whole day.

HUKProp is not the classic multi-week desk. On the English page we read on 2 November 2026, the challenges are one day long, one stage, netting only, and the only instrument is XAU/USD. They show three plans: Spark, a nominal 3K at $30; Ignite, a nominal 5K at $50; Blaze, a nominal 10K at $70. Next to the price they write that profit is paid out at 5×, 6×, or 7× the profit earned from trading. A separate rule says the payout amount is calculated from the trade with the highest profit. Read those two lines together. This is not the usual keep-80-percent-of-the-day deal. They also say the challenge account is a demo, that they open the same position in the real market with more capital, and that your payout comes from that real profit. The reason they give for paying at all is blunt: the trades help train an AI trader they later want to offer cheaply to the same users. The page had Terms, Privacy, and Telegram support. It did not show a legal company name or a licence.

  • Shared rules on that page: one trading day, and trading stops when it ends; the challenge starts at the first login; you must start within five days of purchase; netting mode only; XAU/USD only; one stage; exceeding the trade count or the max-loss limit fails you.
  • The profit payout amount is calculated based on the trade with the highest profit. A limited-availability counter was on the plans and will move, so do not treat it as stock.

✔ What is easy to like

  • The sticker is low, and the rules fit on one screen if you actually read them.
  • One market, one day, one stage. No twelve-pair menu to get lost in.
  • The lot cap and the trade count are written up front, not buried in a novel.

⚠ What should make you pause

  • A nominal 3K with a 0.03 lot cap is not a normal 3K account. The size on the card is not the size of the trade.
  • 5×, 6× and 7× are easy to mistake for a profit split. Tied to the single best trade, they are a different machine.
  • No legal name and no licence on the homepage we read. The track record is not in the same drawer as FTMO or Topstep.
  • Your trades, by their own words, are fuel for an AI trader. Know that before you click pay.

🔎 Look again before you pay: Both payout sentences, the lot cap, and the terms page, the day you pay.

FTMO

Forex and CFDs · Prague, around since 2015

The old public rulebook. A static loss ceiling on the classic challenge, not a trailing one.

FTMO is the Prague desk people already knew before prop ads filled every feed. The famous path is two steps, and there is a one-step path too. This is forex and CFDs, not Chicago futures. On the standard challenge the max-loss habit is a fixed ceiling: your profit does not drag the floor up behind you. It is rarely the cheapest door, which is exactly why bargain hunters usually walk past it.

✔ What is easy to like

  • The rulebook has been public for years, so a breach can be argued against a text, not a mood.
  • Familiar rooms: MetaTrader 4 and 5, cTrader, and TradingView, depending on the program.
  • A static ceiling is easier to picture if trailing drawdown makes you nervous.

⚠ What should make you pause

  • The entry fee usually sits above the younger desks.
  • Minimum trading days mean one lucky ticket does not pass you.
  • News rules and the country list have moved before. Read them the day you pay.

🔎 Look again before you pay: Fee, split, and whether your country is still on the list.

The5ers

Forex · Around since 2016

Built around growing an account, not only surviving a two-step postcard.

The5ers has been around since about 2016, with more than one way in: a bootcamp-style grind and a higher-stakes challenge, depending on the year you look. The story they like to tell is scaling, and a split that can rise once the account grows up. Some programs are kinder about the clock than a classic 30-day sprint. The part people skip is the payout cap per cycle. A pretty split with a low cap is a smaller paycheck than the banner suggests.

✔ What is easy to like

  • A long public life, which is rarer than a loud discount.
  • Scaling is the point of the house, not a footnote.
  • Some paths do not trap you in a short calendar.

⚠ What should make you pause

  • A payout cap can make a high split less generous than it sounds.
  • Several programs means you can buy the wrong personality by accident.
  • Forex only. If you wanted equity index futures, this is the wrong room.

🔎 Look again before you pay: Which program you clicked, its cap, and the current split table.

Visit site · the5ers.com

E8 Markets

Forex and CFDs · Around since 2021

Know the loss type before you fall in love with the account size.

E8 Markets has been in the CFD crowd since about 2021, with a one-step and more classic shapes. The trap is assuming every E8 plan loses money the way FTMO's classic challenge does. Some of their plans use a relative or trailing idea rather than a static floor. That is not evil. It is just a different sport, and it should be chosen on purpose. If you buy the size and ignore the floor, the floor will introduce itself later.

✔ What is easy to like

  • A one-step path when you do not want two exams.
  • Enough time in public that it is not a pop-up shop.
  • The plan menu is honest about being a menu, if you actually open it.

⚠ What should make you pause

  • Relative and trailing loss punishes a different kind of day than a static cap.
  • The brand is one word and the rules are many. Do not blend them in your head.
  • Fees and splits move. We are not freezing a price here.

🔎 Look again before you pay: Static, relative, or trailing — on the exact plan in your cart.

Visit site · e8markets.com

Apex Trader Funding

Futures evaluations · Futures desk, promo-heavy

The discount is the advertisement. The trail and the payout cap are the product.

Apex Trader Funding is a futures evaluation desk famous for loud discounts. A 90 percent off banner is a marketing costume. Under it you still get a trailing drawdown and payout rules that have been revised more than once. Cheap months tempt people to buy a stack of accounts. That is how a sale becomes expensive. If you already trade futures and you can recite the current trail without looking, Apex is a rational seat. If the banner is the reason, close the tab.

✔ What is easy to like

  • Promos can make a futures eval cheaper than the sticker you remember.
  • A known futures workflow, not a forex account in disguise.
  • Enough public argument that payout complaints are not a secret.

⚠ What should make you pause

  • The trail and the payout rules have changed before. They can change again.
  • A stack of cheap accounts is still a stack of fees.
  • Not a forex or gold-on-MT5 product, no matter the ad you clicked.

🔎 Look again before you pay: Today's trail, the payout cap, and how many accounts you are really allowed.
